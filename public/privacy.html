Privacy

Last updated 27 October 2026

Codebase Detective is a practice tool for code-comprehension interviews. This page says what it stores, where it goes and what it does not do.

What we store

  • Account: your name and email address. For email sign-up, a salted scrypt hash of your password (never the password). For Google sign-in, only the name and verified email Google returns; we do not store Google tokens.
  • Your rounds: the round in progress (your code edits, written answers, notes, AI conversation and timings) and a short history of finished rounds with their scores.
  • Cookie: one essential, HttpOnly session cookie that keeps you signed in for 7 days. There are no advertising or analytics cookies and no trackers.
  • In your browser: a cached copy of your current round so a reload does not lose work.

Who receives data

  • Groq receives your questions to the AI coach, the code you are working on, and, when you submit, your answers, diff and notes so a report can be written. Groq processes this under its own terms.
  • Upstash (database) and Vercel (hosting and the isolated sandbox that runs the hidden tests) process the data above on our behalf.
  • Google receives a request only if you choose Sign in with Google. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.
  • We do not sell data or share it with anyone else.

Your choices

Starting a new interview from a report page discards that round's saved work and notes; a one-line history entry (case name, date, score) remains. To have your account and all stored rounds deleted, open an issue at the project repository from the email address you signed up with.

Security

Passwords are hashed, traffic uses HTTPS, API keys stay on the server, and your code runs in your browser or in a network-less sandbox. No system is perfectly secure; do not paste real secrets into the editor or the AI chat.
